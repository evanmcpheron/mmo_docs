Crafting & commissions

Mechanics, governing rules, edge cases and acceptance criteria.

Direct crafting lifecycle

Select recipe → preview exact output and costs → validate recipe/station/skill/slots → quote inventory versions → accept → reserve inputs → run channel or job → atomically consume inputs, create result and grant profession progress → deliver → acknowledge.

A job has a stable ID and state: quoted, reserved, in_progress, committed, delivered, cancelled or failed. A retry returns the existing result for its idempotency key. It never creates a second item or grants another skill point.

Short personal crafts reserve inputs at acceptance and commit at channel completion. Cancellation before commit releases the reservation and grants no output or skill. A server crash before commit releases or resumes the reservation according to the stored state. A crash after commit redelivers the existing result. No outcome depends on whether the client saw the final animation.

Customer commissions

Support public fixed-tip orders and private addressed orders. A customer chooses exact blueprint, allowed configuration, required result grade, recipient, supplied materials, permitted crafter-supplied materials, maximum commission and expiry. Default expiry is 48 hours. A quote clearly shows which costs are not supplied and cannot be exceeded after acceptance.

Public orders do not require blind bidding. Crafters browse eligible work and claim one through an atomic lease. A ten-minute renewable lease is an initial default; a worker may not reserve unlimited orders. The contract cannot be altered after the crafter begins. A cancelled or expired uncommitted order returns customer escrow to a delivery container.

A customer-bound challenge component stays owned by the customer inside escrow. It is usable only for that contract. The final item is created directly for the customer; the crafter receives commission and legitimate profession progress. The crafter never gains a tradable copy, appearance unlock or salvage right merely by performing the job.

Recrafting and service work

Recrafting references one exact item instance and version. The item is unavailable for equip, market, mail or trade during the job. Commit updates that instance, increments its version and records the old/new definition and enhancement decisions. An optimistic-version conflict aborts before consumption.

Enchanting, socket replacement, repair, instrument configuration and similar services use the same contract machinery. The customer previews the result and any destroyed enhancement. Never implement these as a series of unprotected direct trades.

Settlement invariants

For a successful job, inputs are consumed once, output is created once, commission is moved once, fees are destroyed once, and skill progress is awarded once. Total currency changes by exactly the declared fee. Every consumed material quantity belongs to one valid reservation.

Database transactions and constraints enforce local integrity. Use stable item IDs, unique operation keys, nonnegative quantities and a versioned owner/container record. A transactional outbox records committed deliveries and notifications alongside the settlement. A network acknowledgment is not proof of database commit. PostgreSQL's isolation and constraint documentation informs this design; serialization failures require retries of the whole transaction. See Sources S4(sources.html#s4) and Sources S5(sources.html#s5).

Inventory-full and disconnect behavior

A full bag redirects committed output to durable delivery; it does not roll back only half the craft. A disconnected customer may receive a completed order. A disconnected crafter cannot continue an ordinary short-channel craft unless a persisted asynchronous job type explicitly allows it. Initial launch crafts are short synchronous jobs; long offline manufacturing is deferred.

Claiming a delivery validates ownership and item version and transfers it atomically. Repeated claims return the prior status. A notification can be lost without losing the item.

Fraud and exploit controls

Log recipe version, material provenance, crafter, customer, item lineage, commission, mastery snapshot and operation key. Rate-limit quote/claim/cancel operations. Detect repeated self-dealing or alt orders for analytics, but do not ban ordinary crafting for one's own alts merely because it is efficient. There are no commission-specific infinite skill or currency rewards to farm.

Never refund consumed inputs after a result was committed unless an administrative reversal also removes or compensates the output through an audited operation. Support staff cannot edit a database quantity without provenance. Large exploit incidents need a reconciliation tool, not an indiscriminate rollback of unrelated players.

Acceptance tests

Two crafters claim the same order; buyer cancels as crafter commits; inventory fills mid-channel; the server crashes before and after commit; a bound core is offered to a different contract; a recipe is retired during a job; a material-saving bonus changes; the client retries settlement 100 times. The correct result is one valid settlement or no settlement, never duplication or disappearing escrow.

Follow a type name to its canonical members, properties, constraints and review decisions. Definitions are generated from one shared registry.
