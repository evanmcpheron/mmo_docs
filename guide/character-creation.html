Character creation & beginner area

Player-facing flow from a World of Warcraft-style character creator through a guided level 1–10 starting area.

The first player journey runs from a World of Warcraft-style character creation screen into a guided beginner starting area that takes a new character from level 1 to level 10. This is also the first complete playable experience to build and use as a testing ground.

Character creation experience

The character creation screen should feel very similar to World of Warcraft in its overall layout, presentation and flow. Make the character the visual focus, surround it with clear race, class and appearance choices, and let the player understand their future character before entering the world. Present this in the game's own pixel-art style, with its own artwork and interface wording.

Use a large central character preview, faction/race/class choices to one side, and appearance options to the other. Keep the selected character's name and the final creation button easy to find. Changing a choice updates the preview immediately. Players can rotate the preview through the available sprite directions and see how their starting outfit looks.

  1. Start a new character. From character selection, choose Create Character to open the creation screen.
  2. Choose a faction and race. Show faction choices clearly, followed by their available races. Each race has a portrait, name and brief description of its gameplay differences. Humans can join either faction with identical racial attributes. Keep faction and race as distinct choices.
  3. Choose a class. Show class icons, a short description of how each class plays, its possible roles and a preview of its specialization options. Specialization is chosen at level 10. Unavailable choices remain visible with a clear explanation: Paladin and Cleric belong to the good side; Necromancer and Warlock belong to the evil side. Every race within a faction can choose all of that faction's available classes.
  4. Customize appearance. Offer the available body, skin, face, hair and color options for the selected race, plus randomize. Keep the character preview visible throughout. Allow players to revisit earlier choices without losing compatible appearance settings. If a faction or race change makes another choice unavailable, explain the change before clearing that choice.
  5. Name and review. Show the selected faction, race, class, appearance and name together. Explain naming requirements beside the name field. If the name is unavailable or invalid, keep all other choices and let the player correct it.
  6. Create and enter. Confirm the character, show it on the character-selection screen, and make Enter World the clear next action. Entering places the level-1 character at the beginner area's safe arrival point with the first guide nearby. If creation fails, retain the player's choices and show how to try again.

Keep the main decisions easy to understand. Put detailed attribute comparisons in optional information panels. The race and faction rules still apply, and appearance does not change combat reach or movement speed.

Beginner starting area: levels 1–10

Every new character begins in a dedicated beginner area with a clear path to level 10. It should feel like a small, complete opening adventure: a safe arrival, nearby tasks, gradually tougher fights and a clear departure into the wider game. Introduce one idea at a time through short instructions and immediate practice.

Use a common starter-area design with equivalent faction-separated versions, so all races and classes receive the same learning opportunities. A separate starting zone for every race is unnecessary for this first experience. Keep the area free of hostile player combat. The required path is completable alone by every available class; playing with friends is optional.

The area needs a safe quest hub, nearby combat spaces, practice targets, a vendor, a recovery point, an optional gathering/crafting introduction and a marked exit. Objectives lead naturally between these places. Keep travel short, make the current objective easy to find, and show where to return after completing it. Location names, story dialogue and a detailed map can be authored later.

Completing the required introductory tasks and their normal encounters must provide enough experience to reach level 10 before the final challenge. Optional crafting, grouping and side tasks are extra activities; skipping them must not leave the player short of level 10. Avoid requiring repeated creature grinding to finish the introduction.

Teach abilities at their existing unlock levels of 1, 3, 5, 7 and 9. Keep instructions short and let players revisit help when needed. If a player dies, show a clear route back into the current task. Leaving the game and returning should resume the current objective and preserve completed tasks.

Level-10 graduation and handoff

At level 10, the guide introduces specialization as the next major choice. Show each option's role, playstyle and three starting specialization abilities. Preview a suggested allocation for the nine saved specialization points and apply it only after confirmation. The character has earned nine class points and nine specialization points by this stage; completing the beginner area grants no extra talent points.

Give the player a nearby practice opportunity with the chosen specialization, then a short final encounter with a clear danger to avoid and a reason to use the new abilities. Adapt its objectives to the chosen role, including friendly targets for healing where needed. It must be completable alone by tank, healer and damage specializations. It introduces the role without requiring a dungeon group.

After the introductory tasks, level-10 specialization choice and final challenge are complete, give a useful completion reward and a clear quest or travel prompt into the next leveling area. Tell the player where they are going and whom to meet. Let them finish optional beginner activities before leaving. Practice activities can be repeated without awarding the one-time completion rewards again.

A testing ground for development

Build this opening journey as the first complete playable experience before expanding the wider world. Its compact size should make it easy to create fresh characters and repeatedly walk through character creation, quests, combat, leveling, equipment and the level-10 specialization handoff.

Keep practice targets, short combat encounters, a vendor, introductory profession activities and the final challenge close enough to revisit quickly. Use fresh characters to replay the full journey; let existing starter characters repeat practice encounters. Begin with the classes currently playable, then repeat the journey for every available race, faction and class as they are added.

  • A new player can understand the creation choices, correct a name or change a selection, and enter the world as the character they reviewed.
  • The required solo path reaches level 10 with useful equipment and understandable objectives, without depending on side tasks or finding a group.
  • Each new ability and talent choice has a nearby opportunity to be used. Both factions receive equivalent learning and progression.
  • Death, full bags, shared quest targets and leaving/returning do not leave the player unable to continue.
  • The player can choose a specialization, practice its role, finish the final challenge and follow a clear next step into the wider game.

These are player-experience goals for the feature. The area is ready for broader expansion when a fresh character can complete this whole journey and the same space supports convenient repeat playtests.
