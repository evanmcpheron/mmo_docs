Profession system

Mechanics, governing rules, edge cases and acceptance criteria.

Design objective

Professions must produce useful, obtainable results while the character is still in the relevant level band. Max-level profitability alone does not satisfy this requirement. Gathering, crafting, service work and trade should be valid activities throughout progression.

Blizzard's published WoW: Forever design describes added recipes across level ranges and profession-created campsite utilities. That is the reference principle used here—not a reproduction of its recipe list, class bonuses or formulas. See source S1. The specific rules below are this project's proposed design.

Profession slots

Choose two manufacturing professions and two gathering professions. Cooking, Fishing and First Aid are available to everyone as common life skills. Manufacturing and gathering do not compete for the same two slots; otherwise a player who wants two crafts is pushed toward gathering alts immediately.

Manufacturing: choose two Main contribution
Blacksmithing Metal weapons, mail/plate components, shields and maintenance
Leatherworking Leather armor, flexible components and reinforcement
Tailoring Cloth armor, cloaks, bags and textile components
Alchemy Recovery consumables, preparations and catalysts
Enchanting Item enhancements, magical service work and salvage processing
Engineering Bounded devices, weapon attachments, tools and field stations
Woodworking Bows, staves, shields, instruments and structural utility items
Jewelcrafting Rings, necklaces, gems and profession accessories
Inscription Ability presentation glyphs, situational scrolls and enhancement patterns
Gathering: choose two Main contribution
Mining Ore, stone and occasional gems
Herbalism Plants, fibers and reagents
Skinning Hides and creature-derived materials from eligible kills
Forestry Timber, resin and wood components

Cooking supplies recovery food and prepared meals. Fishing supplies ingredients and useful material byproducts. First Aid supplies emergency bandages and out-of-combat treatment. These three have depth but do not displace a party healer.

There are 16 profession definitions in the reference. This is a complete system roster, not a requirement to ship all professions before the combat prototype is stable.

Independent skill progression

Each profession has skill 1–300 across six 50-point bands. Profession skill is not the same as character level, and combat talent points cannot be spent on it. A low-combat-level specialist can craft advanced goods using acquired materials and stations; using those goods still requires their item level. No high-level item ignores its equip requirement because the character crafted it personally.

Crafting band Skill Intended item users Basic recipes available
I 1–50 Levels 1–9 Immediately upon training
II 51–100 Levels 10–19 At skill 51
III 101–150 Levels 20–29 At skill 101
IV 151–200 Levels 30–39 At skill 151
V 201–250 Levels 40–49 At skill 201
VI 251–300 Levels 50–60 At skill 251; level-60 finishes use late-band recipes

Band recipes open at offsets 0, 15, 30 and 45 within the skill band. A complete band should include a immediately useful entry product, a consumable or repeat-demand output, a useful upgrade and a service/tool output. Different professions fulfill these through their own product families; Woodworking's repeat demand includes staff/bow parts, replacement enhancements and market commissions rather than forcing bows to disappear.

Deterministic skill gain

A successful, eligible craft grants skill-progress units based on current_skill − recipe_required_skill: 100 for a gap below 10; 50 for 10–19; 20 for 20–29; zero at 30 or more. Every 100 units grants one skill, carrying the remainder. Recipe output quality does not randomly fail. Craft cancellation grants no progress. Refunds, duplicated requests, salvage and material transfers grant none.

Gathering grants the same difficulty-relative progress on successful node claims. Common low-tier gathering remains profitable when there is demand, but does not level skill 300 indefinitely. Training has a predictable affordable fee, not a hard character-level gate.

Use first-craft bonuses only as optional exploration rewards, not as a limited weekly power currency. No daily knowledge cap, time-gated energy meter or random catch-up lottery is required for the initial system.

Useful while leveling: enforceable tests

A learner using normally available materials should reach a useful entry recipe in a band before character progression makes its products obsolete. The target is less than 30 minutes from entering a character-level band to producing or commissioning one useful item, excluding a deliberately rare prestige recipe. This is a playtest target, not a measured duration.

At levels 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50, test a character using ordinary quest rewards and ask whether a same-band craft improves an actual slot, meaningfully changes a stat distribution, or supplies useful utility. At least two of those three conditions should hold for every manufacturing profession's customers.

Basic recipes and essential tools come from training or ordinary vendors. Advanced patterns may come from group activities, research tasks and player trade. A rare recipe cannot be the only useful output for levels 10–19. High-level blueprint ownership cannot be required to sell basic band-I recovery supplies.

The starter test must include a player with no rich main character funding them. Materials accessible only through a high-level player's bank do not prove level-appropriate usefulness.

Fixed products, specialist configurations

Use deterministic recipes. The crafting UI displays exact material requirements, result grade, item budget, optional configuration, binding and recipient before commitment. There is no random “quality 1–5” ladder in this baseline.

Standard, Refined, Masterwork and Challenge grades are explicit blueprints or upgrade paths, not unpredictable outcomes of the same recipe. Advanced gear can be made through customer-provided bound components, allowing a dedicated non-raiding crafter to provide the service without personally acquiring the customer's raid progress.

Specialists unlock alternate configurations, faster handling and modest efficiency. They do not receive a private combat stat bonus merely for holding the profession. All combat-relevant crafted benefits must be tradable or commissionable under the same eligibility rules for non-crafters.

Profession mastery trees

Each profession earns one mastery point per ten skill levels, up to 30 at skill 300. These points are independent of the character's 118 combat points. Each profession has three branches, each containing five three-rank nodes: 45 available ranks, 30 points to spend. Branches are named and scoped on the profession pages.

Ranks improve a concrete production concern: eligible common-material efficiency, batch handling, specialist configurations, gathering selectivity, station utility or service convenience. Universal ceilings are 12% saved common materials, 15% faster gathering/crafting channels and 10% additional common gathering yield. Rare/earned cores are never saved or duplicated. Output quantity bonuses cannot apply to another bonus output or salvaged material.

Every successful craft consumes at least one of each required non-optional material. Use deterministic remainder accounting for fractional efficiency rather than resetting rounding on every one-item batch. Show savings in the quote. Mastery bonuses are snapshotted and authorized at craft acceptance; changing profession mid-craft cannot upgrade its result.

Changing mastery is free at an appropriate station while idle. Changing the active profession is a separate commitment: a 24-hour activation cooldown, no switching during active work, and profession-bound active benefits removed on deactivation. Previously learned skill and recipes remain dormant rather than being destroyed. This allows correction without turning all professions into instantly interchangeable loadouts. Alts remain possible; do not pretend this policy prevents every account-level specialization advantage.

Materials and old-tier relevance

Use six material bands. Advanced recipes primarily consume current-band materials, with selected previous-band common components in reinforcement, catalysts, thread, resin, maintenance and consumables. Do not require a level-60 crafter to repeat every earlier refinement chain for every item.

The example recipe catalog includes explicit material IDs and quantities, not just “some ore.” Salvage returns common materials at a loss and never rare bound cores. Common starter recovery alternatives remain vendor-accessible with non-exportable starter supplies when necessary, protecting new players from shortages without enabling vendor-arbitrage loops.

Old-tier relevance comes from leveling customers, profession tools, maintained utility products, enhancement replacement and a modest amount of cross-tier demand. It does not come from making every endgame consumable require an enormous quantity of starter-zone resources.

Gathering and shared access

Ordinary nodes have a finite server-owned charge count; start with three charges and a three-second gather channel. Claiming a charge is atomic at channel completion. A losing concurrent claimant receives a clear empty-node response and consumes no tool item or materials. Each character has a per-node respawn-cycle claim limit. Respawn timing and population density are server data, not a client timer.

Skinning requires eligible loot rights and a completed loot phase. Unclaimed carcasses become public after a configurable grace period. It cannot consume a body reserved for an encounter mechanic. Fishing resolves from an authorized cast/interaction session with bounded timing tolerance; no client reports the catch item.

Danger and PvP rules are independent of profession skill. Protected starter resource areas remain protected. Node competition must not make a required low-tier profession path effectively unavailable on one faction.

Stations and field utility

Every manufacturing profession has a public full-function station. Portable field stations provide limited convenience: repair access, ordinary band-appropriate crafting or out-of-combat preparation. They cannot block navigation, provide combat collision, be placed on objectives or exceed an owner object limit.

A field site permits three utility objects and one active preparation buff per category. Effects are weaker or equal substitutes for ordinary class preparation, never additive mandatory raid buffs. Activation takes five out-of-combat seconds. Both factions receive mechanically equivalent utilities. A missing crafter never prevents a party from repairing or leaving an instance.

Economic and balance risks

Two manufacturing slots do not guarantee interdependence when a population has many alts. Monitor product demand, market concentration and actual production costs. Avoid profession-exclusive raid DPS bonuses, infinite-use superior bombs and rare-ingredient monopolies. Engineering devices share consumable cooldown categories and rated-PvP restrictions.

Professions remain viable through useful goods and services, not by forcing players to destroy good equipment repeatedly or perform daily chores. Mature-server demand is tested separately from fresh-server leveling demand.
