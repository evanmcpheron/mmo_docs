Combat mechanics

Mechanics, governing rules, edge cases and acceptance criteria.

Targeting and movement

Support target lock, aimed line/cone, ground point, self area, friendly target, legal movement and bounded pet commands. These are targeting methods; projectile travel is an independent execution property. A target-locked spell may launch a projectile, and a ground effect may follow a delayed impact.

Default movement is 3.5 world units per second. Normalize diagonal keyboard input. A player may move in one direction while aiming elsewhere. Eight-direction artwork does not restrict authoritative aim to eight angles. Base walk speed, collision radius and attack reach are the same across races.

Click selects a visible eligible entity. Target cycling sorts eligible enemies in the facing hemisphere by angular difference, distance and stable entity ID. It excludes dead, hidden, friendly, invulnerable and cross-floor entities unless the current ability explicitly permits them. Friendly frame targeting has precedence over characters hidden behind scenery. Ground-cast modes support preview/confirm and optional quick cast with a clear cancel input.

These defaults are not a license to stamp every ability with identical parameters. The profile catalog supplies a complete baseline; bespoke variants require explicit overrides and tests.

Cast lifecycle

At acceptance, verify learned ability, selected build version, faction/class ownership, equipment, alive/control state, target relation, floor, range, line of sight and available resources. Reserve the cost. Start the cast and GCD. A client may predict the cast indicator but may not authorize a hit.

At release, verify the conditions declared in revalidation_policy, consume the reserved cost, start the ability cooldown and create the authoritative effect or projectile. A cancelled or interrupted pre-release cast releases its reservation, leaves the started GCD in place and does not consume the ability cooldown unless that ability explicitly has a start-commit policy. Channels commit cost at start and do not refund completed or remaining ticks on cancellation. Their tooltips must say so.

Movement cancels stationary casts. A talent may permit movement only through a precise flag or window. Do not let visual animation events decide whether a cast finished. During a cast, changing target does not retarget the in-progress spell unless explicitly allowed.

An accepted instant ability commits cost and cooldown in the same simulation transaction. A target becoming invalid before acceptance costs nothing. A valid launched attack that later hits a wall or loses an eligible target does not refund its cost.

Projectiles and ground effects

Every damaging projectile has a server ID, owner, lifetime, speed, hit policy, floor and collision mask. Cosmetic client projectiles can be corrected or cancelled. Server collision uses swept segments to avoid fast objects skipping thin walls.

A homing projectile validates relation and target existence at impact, and follows its declared wall-collision policy. It cannot damage a character who became an ally. A straight skillshot uses its original direction and does not acquire a new target silently. Piercing attacks maintain a set of already hit entities and a finite target count.

Ground effects have a placement range, radius, floor, duration, tick interval, target cap, ownership and overlap rule. Enforce legal placement before spending resources. They cannot spawn beyond a closed door, in a protected spawn area, on an inaccessible floor or inside forbidden navigation volumes.

Friendly damage is disabled in the baseline. An offensive area effect selects only legal hostile entities at the time of each tick. Beneficial effects respect faction and group rules even if the recipient entered the visual area.

Tanking and threat

A tank specialization grants a 3.0× threat multiplier on its own damage, a single-target challenge at level 10 and basic active mitigation. Taunt is not an optional talent. Single-target challenge sets threat to 110% of the current highest eligible threat and forces attention for three seconds; cooldown eight seconds. It cannot engage an unengaged distant boss, affect a player, or defeat an encounter immunity.

Ordinary damage grants threat equal to effective damage. Effective healing creates 0.5 threat per healing point, divided among engaged NPCs that can perceive the event. Shield prevention follows the same 0.5 rule when consumed. Overkill and overhealing do not generate extra threat. Summon damage belongs to its declared pet threat owner; no double credit to pet and summoner.

An NPC changes ordinary aggro when another eligible target exceeds current threat by 10% in melee or 30% outside melee. Scripted encounter targets may temporarily override this. After a mechanic ends, the normal table resumes. Leashing clears threat and resets encounter-owned hazards; it never awards kill rewards or refunds spent consumables.

Healer and support interaction

Direct healing, sustained healing, absorb prevention and emergency recovery are different responsibilities. No mandatory party mechanic requires a named exclusive class. Shared healer training covers revival and a configurable cleanse; class specialization determines efficiency and pattern.

For group offensive buffs, use a non-stacking category cap. Start at a 10% maximum combined bonus from the ordinary group-amplification category, with most effects providing 3–5%. A second support character must contribute their own output and complementary utility, not multiply a permanent exponential buff chain.

Combat logs record personal damage, effective healing, absorbs consumed, damage transferred and attributed offensive support separately. Contribution attribution is an accounting model, not additional damage. When a 5% buff changes 1,000 baseline damage into 1,050, attribute at most the incremental 50 to support while retaining 1,050 as actual target damage. Multiple buffs split the measured increment through a documented normalized weighting; never credit the full increase to each provider.

Death, recovery and battle revival

At zero health, atomically stop casts, end controllable effects, invalidate pet commands, settle damage ledgers and mark the character dead. Drop no equipment or inventory on ordinary death. Apply a modest repair cost through durability loss, not item deletion. Do not incur durability damage in normalized rated PvP.

Out-of-combat revival takes eight seconds and restores 20% health and mana. Healers receive it through role training at level 10. A five-player boss attempt has one shared battle-revival charge; a 10-player attempt has two; larger attempts cap at three. Charges reset only when the encounter genuinely resets or completes. Individual cooldowns and the shared pool both apply. Disconnect/reconnect does not create a new charge.

Solo recovery uses ordinary food, drink, rest and modest class self-sustain. Professions improve preparation and convenience but are not required to recover from a death or a empty resource bar.

What a group encounter must test

Use one encounter that combines a tank-facing cone, an interruptible cast, a recoverable group-damage pulse, a moving hazard and a short priority-target phase. Test the same fight with each common tank and healer, without a faction-exclusive class and without an optimized Bard composition. This reveals whether talents genuinely change decisions while the core encounter remains completable.
