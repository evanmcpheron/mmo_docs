Objective and scope

Prove the selected visual direction on a pinned Godot build before committing to asset volume.

Scope: A small standalone scene, low-resolution orthographic world view, pixel-stable camera, outline/toon material candidates and renderer/pass feasibility; production assets and final environment effects follow separately. Holland sets the visual target; Roystan’s Unity tutorial informs edge detection, with Godot-specific depth, normal-space, projection, sampling and composition behavior tested explicitly.

Acceptance criteria

  1. AC 1

    A packaged reference scene records engine/export revision, renderer, graphics backend and test hardware; required depth/normal and screen-copy access is demonstrated, with any unsupported path or engine patch explicitly resolved rather than assumed from the article.

  2. AC 2

    The orthographic view has a declared internal resolution, framing, pixel scale, nearest presentation and aspect/minimum-window policy; resizing and high-DPI displays retain readable separate UI and consistent world pixels.

  3. AC 3

    Slow and diagonal camera tracking, stationary views and moving actors have reviewed frame sequences for crawl, shimmer and jitter; camera grid alignment with residual screen correction, or a measured equivalent, preserves smooth tracking without snapping simulation.

  4. AC 4

    A representative model/terrain comparison establishes silhouette outlines one pixel wide at the internal resolution, distinct convex-edge highlights and approved toon lighting at gameplay scale; near/far surfaces, grazing slopes, thin geometry and overlapping actors do not produce filled surfaces or distracting false edges.

  5. AC 5

    Opaque, transparent and screen-reading test objects expose missing/stale outlines, depth and ordering errors; a water/reflection/atmosphere feasibility sample identifies required passes and a tested fallback before those effects enter production.

  6. AC 6

    Review records CPU/GPU frame costs and memory at declared settings, visual acceptance findings and chosen defaults; the scene is reproducible from source, and custom-engine maintenance or platform exclusions require a documented implementation decision.

  7. AC 7

    The visual acceptance baseline uses assets/graphics_concept.png as the principal appearance reference, with annotated gameplay-scale comparisons for character detail relative to simpler environmental props, palette, depth and lighting. Review records observable acceptance bounds and approved deviations for the existing still/motion tests; the concept establishes neither pixel-perfect equivalence nor engine capability, and unresolved blocking visual findings prevent acceptance.

  8. AC 8

    Water feasibility records whether shaders, animation, geometry and particles satisfy the approved visual requirements before proposing physical fluid simulation. The chosen reflection/refraction method and fallback are exercised with off-screen and newly visible objects, moving outlined/transparent objects and synthetic authorized/excluded views; missing information, stale buffers, pass-order artifacts and unsupported renderer paths have tested mitigations or approved visual alternatives on the pinned build.

  9. AC 9

    Ambient-occlusion/contact-depth feasibility evaluates applicable baked, real-time or authored approaches on the pinned renderer and measures their cost and lower-cost fallback. A controllable prototype combines the candidate with water, lighting/shadows, outlines, foliage/transparency, animated actors and atmosphere during stationary, continuous slow and diagonal camera/actor motion; recorded pass composition, silhouette visibility and pixel stability meet the approved acceptance bounds before asset-volume production.
