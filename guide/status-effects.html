Statuses, control & summons

Mechanics, governing rules, edge cases and acceptance criteria.

Status definitions

Complete baseline vocabulary: The existing eight categories also cover explicit crowd control, stealth/concealment, immunity, personal offense, personal defense, movement enhancement and target marks. The canonical StatusDefinition contains a category-to-stacking/refresh matrix. DoT/HoT preserve their next tick with the existing 30% carry cap; shields retain the greater budget; strongest-category effects never add; same-source personal effects replace; control reapplication uses shared diminishing returns. Control names its history group and periodic intervals are positive.

Baseline dispel families are harmful_magic and beneficial_magic; null is explicitly nondispellable. Poison/disease flavor adds no implicit family. Level-ten healer training supplies a harmful_magic module. Per-ability status records, visual assets and actual refresh/cleanup execution remain future content work.

Persistence is a composable object with required cleanup_on_death, cleanup_on_logout and checkpoint_for_transfer flags. Death or logout cleanup takes precedence over transfer. A transferred surviving status preserves expiry, the next tick and remaining effect budget; a status not checkpointed is removed on transfer. Exact enabled per-status category, refresh, stacking and cleanse bindings remain required authored content.

Every status has a stable ID, source entity, owner ability, category, duration, periodic interval if any, maximum stacks, dispel family, PvP override, refresh policy, exclusivity group and persistence policy. A status without these fields is invalid content, not an invitation for implementation-specific defaults.

Periodic refresh carries at most 30% of the base duration, preserving the next scheduled tick. Recasting a periodic effect never creates an extra immediate tick. The source power snapshot is captured on application, but temporary group-amplification buffs are evaluated per tick; this avoids retaining an expired party burst buff for a long DoT. Refreshing updates the snapshot for future ticks only.

Dispel removes one eligible instance unless the ability explicitly states otherwise. Server priority is harmful severity category, shortest remaining dangerous effect, then stable ID. PvP dispel backlash must have a declared ceiling and cannot trigger another backlash recursively. A failed cleanse on a target with no eligible debuff consumes neither cost nor cooldown at acceptance.

Within a diminishing-return group, durations progress through 100%, 50%, 25%, then immunity. Reset the group 18 seconds after the most recent effect ends. As an additional anti-chain rule, a player cannot spend more than six seconds in hard control in a rolling 15-second interval; shorten a newly applied effect to the remaining allowance. Roots have an eight-second rolling allowance. Reapplying another source does not reset this history.

Normal bosses are immune to ordinary hard control and displacement. Interruptible boss actions must be explicitly flagged. A class whose resource depends on root or stun gets its authored non-control alternative against immune bosses; it must not generate an unlimited proc merely by repeatedly failing to control them.

All characters receive the same PvP control-break action in rated modes: 120-second cooldown, removes eligible hard control and grants two seconds of immunity to that same category. This is a mode rule, not a racial power. It does not clear unrelated damage-over-time effects or reset movement cooldowns.

Trigger ordering

A root action carries action_id, effect_sequence, source_entity_id, owner_entity_id, build_version, content_version, parent_event_id and trigger_depth. Process initial effects in authored order and targets in stable entity-ID order. Generated effects enter a bounded event queue.

Allow at most depth two and eight triggered child effects per root target event in the baseline. Each trigger declares eligible source tags. Periodic ticks, reflected damage, redirected damage, minion dismissals, empty toggles and cosmetic repeats are excluded from generic on-hit resource triggers unless specifically allowed. A self-trigger may fire only once for a root event. Duplicate network requests never create another root event.

Reflects cannot reflect reflected damage. Heal echoes cannot echo other echoes. Damage redirected to a protector cannot be redirected again. Consuming a disease cannot count its removed remaining damage as both a periodic tick and a burst without explicitly dividing the budget.

Pets, totems and formations

The authored Bonewright exception is a two-unit specialized_mobile servant. Replacing a lesser servant first releases that servant within the same transaction, then validates the final shared capacity. It never creates a separate summon pool or a second full aggregate damage budget.

A character has one four-unit summon-capacity budget. A lesser mobile servant costs one, an elite companion costs four, and a normal static combat totem costs one. Specialization-specific allowances select within this budget; class and specialization summon skills do not create separate uncapped pools. A cosmetic pet costs zero and has no combat behavior.

At equal equipment, the full capacity pool receives one authored aggregate damage budget, divided across entities. Four servants do not each inherit the same full-strength coefficient as an elite pet. Totem links are evaluated from owner-defined pairs, not all possible pairs: maximum three active links and four controlled objects. Replacement removes the old object before creating the new one.

Default pet commands are follow, hold, attack my eligible target and return. Aggressive free-roaming mode is omitted. Pets cannot discover enemies outside the owner's permitted information set. Combat recall follows normal path and control rules; out-of-combat leash recovery may teleport a stuck pet after five seconds. Never use that recovery to teleport a pet across a wall into combat.

On disconnect, pets stop accepting commands and follow the persisted owner-disconnect policy. Offensive auras and unattended summons end after the grace period; no offline combat farming. A zone transfer transfers or reconstructs one owner budget, not a second independent set.

Persistence policy

Store long cooldown expiry as absolute authoritative time. Store combat status snapshots and damage ledgers with the session's ownership token. Short combat events are not individual database writes; checkpoints and recovery policy govern them. Death, logout and graceful transfer have explicit cleanup. An unclean restart applies a conservative encounter reset and restores durable inventory, not a fabricated exact replay of every lost combat tick.

Required tests

Recast a DoT one millisecond before a tick; attempt repeated no-target heals; reflect a reflect; redirect into a redirect; dismiss and resummon under a death-trigger talent; switch forms with delayed damage; reconnect during a dispel; change party during a beneficial aura. All must preserve caps, attribution and ownership.
