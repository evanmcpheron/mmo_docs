Objective and scope

Provide isolated group-owned encounter spaces with controlled admission, departure and reset.

Scope: Runtime instance identity, party admission, protected entry/exit, encounter locks and instance cleanup in a single process. Dungeon/raid content and separate-process scheduling remain separate.

Acceptance criteria

  1. AC 1

    Two parties entering the same content receive distinct runtime instances; entities, effects, targeting and chat/interest queries cannot cross the boundary.

  2. AC 2

    Admission checks authoritative membership, capacity, eligibility and encounter state; retries cannot create duplicate occupancy or bypass a lock.

  3. AC 3

    Leaving, disconnecting and rejoining resolve to the configured instance and safe location; an unavailable or closed instance fails safely.

  4. AC 4

    A genuine reset clears encounter-owned hazards, threat, summons and eligibility state without refunding player consumables, resetting cooldown/debt or repeating durable rewards.

  5. AC 5

    Closing an empty or completed instance leaves no orphaned controllable entities or pending ownership claim.

Verification and blockers

Completion evidence required: Required future proof: two-party isolation/admission tests, reconnect and unavailable-instance cases, reset/conservation fixtures and lifecycle diagnostics showing cleanup.

Recorded evidence: None. Required evidence above describes future verification.

Blockers: None recorded; see hard prerequisites.

Ticket planning

Before creating or revising tickets, apply the mandatory reuse assessment. Inspect existing owners and evidence, then map every criterion to the additional work and verification required.

Use BW-081 as the parent epic. Link each future ticket to the AC links above and state the evidence it will produce. Split work into demonstrable behaviors; one criterion may require several tickets. Epic completion still requires every criterion, verified evidence and completed hard prerequisites.

Ordering rationale: Instance isolation is a gameplay/security capability before it is a deployment topology; validate it locally before adding cross-process failure modes.

Related open decisions: Incremental topology and comparable branch orders
