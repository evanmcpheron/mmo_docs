PvP & encounter rules

Mechanics, governing rules, edge cases and acceptance criteria.

PvE composition rules

Use one tank, one healer and three damage players for standard five-player dungeons. Encounter objectives may reward interrupts, control, dispels, movement and support without requiring one named class. Include readable setup time before avoidable lethal mechanics. Do not balance every encounter around stationary maximum damage.

Require tests with common classes only, with no Bard, with a support-oriented damage slot and with different melee/ranged mixes. Healing profiles must support both compact and distributed damage patterns. Tank mechanics must be solvable with frontal blocking, protective magic, form defense and delayed-damage management without making one category categorically superior.

Boss control immunity cannot eliminate a specialization's damage-resource loop. Give control-triggered resources an explicitly budgeted immune-target alternative. Pet users need valid paths, clear command feedback and recall behavior; do not compensate for broken pathfinding by granting pets unrestricted wall crossing.

Faction-exclusive classes

Paladin/Cleric exclusivity creates more healing choices on the good side; Necromancer/Warlock exclusivity adds damage variety on the evil side. That is intentional asymmetry, not a solved balance outcome. Common Warrior, Bard, Druid, Shaman and Monk kits cover mandatory role functions.

Group buffs are category-based and have common providers or weaker ordinary preparation substitutes. A raid cannot require a good-exclusive aura or evil-exclusive portal to meet an unavoidable timer. Do not give a faction a flat compensating damage bonus based on population; use recruitment, matching and access policies instead.

PvP modes

Reviewed first rules: Duels require both participants, use a server boundary and explicit positive invitation/grace/time limits, end nonlethally at one health and grant no competitive reward or resource/cooldown reset. Surrender or boundary timeout forfeits; disconnect forfeits only after the existing disconnect grace. A time-limit tie draws; invalid protection/scope cancels.

The first objective match has two five-player teams and one control_point_v1. Capture requires the configured continuous uncontested presence; opposing eligible presence pauses capture and an empty point clears unfinished capture. Only a present uncontested owner scores each configured tick. First to the configured score limit wins; at match timeout higher score wins and equality draws, with no implicit overtime. Invalidated matches cancel. Capture, scoring, respawn and hostile-participation durations are required explicit ruleset content. Rated normalization remains level 60 under the existing control and talent-budget rules.

Match lifecycle values are preparing, active, completed and cancelled. Teams and the authored ruleset must validate before activation. An authored end condition creates a completed immutable result; cancellation creates no competitive result. Terminal matches cannot restart or grant rewards twice. Objectives, scoring, boundaries and participation timers must be authored and tested before PvP is enabled.

Launch PvP policy: consensual duels, instanced equal-size objective matches and explicitly flagged open-world combat. Open-world faction hostility alone does not permit attacking an unflagged character on a normal ruleset. Healing a flagged participant requires confirmation or explicit automatic-flag settings and then applies the relevant hostile-participation timer. Protected spawn and recovery areas reject offensive actions.

Faction conflict, territory construction and sieges are separate later mechanics; no map or territorial lore is designed here. Rated modes should start small enough to measure composition effects, such as five-versus-five objective matches, before claiming balance across every raid-sized battle.

Gear normalization

Rated PvP uses effective level 60, the same 118-point build budget, normalized primary/health budgets and a controlled secondary-stat budget. The player's selected secondary distribution is preserved proportionally within the normalized ceiling. No permanent extra power comes from profession ownership, racial cooldowns, consumable stacking or uncapped challenge-item procs.

Non-rated open-world PvP retains normal progression, with level-difference indicators and protections for starter areas. Do not use normalization rules in one mode without showing them clearly. Disabled item effects, unavailable consumables and PvP-adjusted durations appear in tooltips before the match starts.

Consumables and control

Rated play excludes ordinary external combat consumables in the first ruleset; match-provided standardized supplies can be added later. Engineering bombs, field stations and profession-only combat gadgets are therefore not a hidden requirement. Food preparation affects out-of-combat recovery only where the mode explicitly permits it.

Use the shared crowd-control diminishing returns, rolling hard-control ceiling and universal mode-specific control break. Melee uptime, ranged line of sight, pet disruption and healer interruption require class-specific testing. The same base ability should remain recognizable in PvE and PvP even when coefficients or duration differ.

Encounter budgets

Define expected encounter length, group composition, movement frequency, unavoidable damage, interrupt frequency and target count before tuning damage coefficients. Suggested first encounter families are: a single-target defense test; a two-target priority switch; a moving hazard fight; a sustained healing test; and a controlled multi-target pull.

Avoid infinite adds as a resource farm, unlimited corpse generation, targets that continuously enter/leave combat to reset cooldowns, and reflected damage triggering encounter rewards. Reward eligibility belongs to the completed encounter ID and participant policy.

Evaluation targets

Compare each specialization's effective output, survival, control, mobility and ally contribution at equal budgets. A provisional 5% sustained-output band can identify outliers, but should not erase intentional burst or utility differences. Match outcomes, pick rates and exclusion rates matter alongside simulations.

Faction win rate alone is not proof of class balance: player skill, queue composition and population can bias it. Compare skill-adjusted matches and matched role compositions, and report uncertainty. No numerical balance target in this reference has been verified through real players.
