Specializations & playstyles

Mechanics, governing rules, edge cases and acceptance criteria.

Three separate design responsibilities

Class sets the shared resource language, equipment permissions, defensive limitations and recognizable core actions. Specialization selects the combat role and primary decision loop. Talents change how the player performs that loop, with an opportunity cost. Gear modifies the result within an item budget; it should not be the only source of interesting behavior.

A Mage becoming a Frostbinder instead of a Pyromancer changes the resource setup, the reason to maintain an effect and the way area control enters a rotation. A Pyromancer choosing sustained combustion instead of ignition burst remains a Pyromancer, but makes different decisions about when to refresh Burning and when to consume it. A two-percent coefficient increase by itself does not satisfy either distinction.

Playstyle-change acceptance rubric

A major specialization or talent route must change at least two of these observable dimensions, including at least one player decision rather than only an output number:

Dimension Observable difference
Ability priority Different next action in an otherwise identical combat state
Resource decisions Different pooling, spending, recovery or risk thresholds
Movement A reason to hold position, advance, retreat or move with allies
Target selection Concentrated, distributed, marked-target, chained or area priority
Timing Planned burst, steady maintenance, reactive defense or anticipatory preparation
Group behavior Personal output versus bounded ally enhancement or protection
Failure mode A distinct consequence when the player makes the wrong decision

The route must have a stated cost: less reach, less burst, more upkeep, shorter duration, weaker individual summons, greater exposure or less direct output. “The same build, but better at everything” is not a legitimate alternative.

Test routes at equal level, equipment budget and encounter conditions. Compare actual action sequences, time moving, target changes, resource waste and effective contribution. An output difference of 3% with identical decisions is mostly a tuning difference, not a new playstyle.

Example 1: one class, different specializations

Pyromancer maintains Burning and builds Heat. Its important decision is whether to preserve future damage or consume setup for a short window. Frostbinder applies Chill and chooses when to trade spatial control for damage. Arcanist manages the rising mana cost of accumulated charges.

The abilities can share targeting geometry and server effect operations without sharing the same combat loop. This is why reusable implementation does not require homogeneous gameplay.

Example 2: two Arcanist routes with a concrete budget

Charge Lance consumes one to four Arcane Charges. Its prototype damage is 0.8P + 0.35P × charges; mana cost is 3% + 2% × charges of maximum mana.

At one charge, the cast deals 1.15P for 5% mana. At four charges, it deals 2.20P for 11% mana. These isolated ratios exclude the time and cost of charge generation and are not rotation-DPS comparisons.

A charged-burst route pools four charges for a vulnerable target or a short boss window. An efficient-circulation route spends fewer charges, uses recovery more often and tolerates frequent movement. The burst player risks interruption and mana shortage; the circulation player gives up peak damage when a short window appears. A talent that allows one mobile follow-up after Warp Step must trade away a stationary bonus or use a meaningful cooldown—it is not free universal movement casting.

Example 3: Necromancer summon choices

A Gravecaller chooses one four-capacity elite servant or up to four one-capacity lesser servants. Both configurations share the same aggregate base damage budget. The elite concentrates attention and is vulnerable to one disabling effect; the squad distributes presence but requires more command management and has fragile individuals.

A squad must not simply provide four times the damage or four independent copies of every on-hit proc. A defensively oriented variant spends part of the aggregate pet budget on interception. Its lower personal damage is an actual cost, not an invisible promise to balance it later.

Example 4: healer decisions

Grovekeeper can let healing seeds mature, or consume them for immediate recovery. Consumption removes the seed and its future healing budget; it cannot deliver an instant heal and still receive all remaining ticks. A reactive build replants more often and uses more mana. A sustained build relies on anticipating damage and may fail to rescue an unprepared target.

Oracle differs at the specialization level: it earns its secondary resource when wards actually prevent damage, rather than when HoTs mature. Both heal, but the timing and failure conditions are different.

Example 5: Bard support choices

Skald's personal route spends more Cadence on melee finishers. Its group route gives up some personal damage and resource time for a limited amplification window. The group route must remain useful without becoming a mandatory fourth party role, and the personal route must remain viable for ordinary solo play.

Attribution reports actual incremental allied damage rather than displaying fabricated personal DPS. Support caps and duplicate-buff rules are visible. A second Bard does not automatically double every improvement.

Early identity, later depth

Specialization identity begins at level 10 with three key abilities and nine banked specialization points. Early transformative choices sit in tier II. Later nodes deepen the selected strategy through controlled interactions, and tier-VI capstones add a more demanding or specialized pattern.

Role necessities are baseline. A tank never needs to buy taunt to function. A healer never needs to complete 40 levels of a tree before having an affordable heal. Talent refunding cannot remove a core role service. Branches alter how the role is performed, not whether the class can participate at all.

Every specialization's two-route contract

The class pages include a core loop, two distinct route descriptions, their tradeoff and an explicit resource/state policy for all 37 specializations. Those route descriptions are playtest requirements, not claims that every inherited talent's bespoke effect has already been coded. The binding status on each node remains visible.

Necromancer / GravecallerAllocate one summon-capacity pool and command coordinated attacks; resources come from successful combat, not dismiss/resummon loops.Single sovereign: one elite controlled servant, fewer commands and concentrated target pressure.

March of bones: up to four lesser servants with divided output and coordinated positional coverage.		Elite control is vulnerable to disabling one pet; a squad costs more attention and has weaker individuals, not four full pet budgets.
Necromancer / PlaguebringerMaintain diseases, collect bounded Contagion from actual ticks and choose between extending, spreading and consuming them.Patient decay: longer diseases with preserved total-per-application budgeting and lower maintenance frequency.

Rapid infection: shorter diseases and faster damage realization at greater mana and refresh pressure.		Long effects lose value on short-lived targets; rapid infection is more demanding and easier to exhaust.
Necromancer / SoulreaperMark one enemy, build Essence through direct and channel attacks, then choose a spender or defensive reserve.Repeated extraction: smaller frequent spends and controlled life drain.

Planned requiem: pool Essence for a telegraphed finisher while retaining enough for a ward.		Frequent spending loses burst; pooling risks interruption and reduces immediate self-protection.
Bard / MinstrelMaintain up to two ordinary song slots, heal targets actively and spend Harmony on group recovery without requiring precise audio timing.Moving chorus: move with allies, use short restorative phrases and frequent song transitions.

Prepared haven: establish longer recovery patterns and hold an emergency finale.		Mobile healing trades peak sustained throughput; prepared healing loses value when allies scatter.
Bard / SkaldGenerate Cadence in melee, then choose personal finishing damage or a temporary group enhancement.Solo skald: more personal weapon output and reduced allied amplification.

Battle chorus: spend more time and Cadence on measurable ally enhancement.		Personal damage scales less with coordinated allies; group support performs poorly when allies cannot act. Neither route requires another player to complete solo content.
Bard / VirtuosoBuild a short sequence of offensive note families, then resolve it into a bounded sonic effect.Focused solo: narrow line attacks and direct single-target final notes.

Echo control: use a field and disruptive notes to support clustered combat.		Narrow attacks lose coverage; echo fields need positioning and cannot recursively repeat their own damage.
Rogue / AssassinPrepare a poison/positioning window and spend Combo Points on a timed finisher rather than attacking from stealth indefinitely.Poison attrition: maintain poison, accept slower damage and use defensive re-entry windows.

Opening burst: commit positional attacks and finish during a short marked weakness.		Poison pressure is slower; burst is weaker when forced out of position or its opening is denied.
Rogue / DuelistAlternate attacks with counters to build Advantage; spend it only when the exchange creates an opportunity.Counter specialist: read incoming attacks and turn a successful parry into a finisher.

Mobile exchanges: build through varied weapon actions and short positional changes.		Counter play loses value without incoming eligible attacks; mobile play gives up the strongest counter reward.
Rogue / SaboteurPlace a small device set, control an approach and trigger it deliberately rather than spawning unrestricted traps.Prepared field: deploy fewer stronger devices and wait for enemies to enter the planned area.

Mobile sabotage: use shorter-lived devices and reposition between activations.		Prepared damage is avoidable by moving away; mobile devices have lower individual impact.
Ranger / MarksmanBuild Aim while stationary and consume it on measured shots; movement is permitted but has an explicit opportunity cost.Anchored marksman: hold position for stronger deliberate shots and planned narrow volleys.

Mobile precision: preserve a limited amount of Aim through brief repositioning at reduced stationary bonuses.		Anchoring is vulnerable to hazards and melee pressure; mobility sacrifices peak stationary damage.
Ranger / BeastmasterBuild Bond with successful owner/pet coordination and spend it on commands, while retaining functional owner attacks.Offensive bond: pet and owner focus one priority target with timed combined attacks.

Guarding bond: use more commands for pet interception and protection while reducing direct pet offense.		Offense loses defensive command availability; guarding loses damage and cannot replace a player tank.
Ranger / PathfinderCombine a ranged attack, a close-range technique and traps with legal repositioning; select safe routes rather than permanent kiting immunity.Ambush route: plan traps and use short melee pressure when the target reaches them.

Running route: prioritize mobile shots and defensive waypoints.		Ambush requires preparation; running shots lose stationary and trap burst value.
Druid / WildguardEnter the tank form, generate Resolve through real defensive work and spend it before large incoming hits.Anchored bear: strong self-defense and controlled area threat while holding position.

Mobile guardian: use defensive movement and ally coverage with reduced stationary mitigation.		Anchoring is less flexible; mobile coverage gives up some personal defense. Humanoid utility does not preserve full tank mitigation.
Druid / FeralBuild Combo Points through claw attacks and decide between immediate finishing damage and maintained bleeds.Bleeding pursuit: maintain bleeds while moving between positional opportunities.

Focused predation: spend more points in short direct windows against one target.		Bleeds are slower on fragile enemies; direct windows require uptime and careful resource pooling.
Druid / GrovekeeperApply growing healing seeds, decide when to consume a mature seed and position recovery areas before group damage.Sustained garden: maintain several HoTs and let them mature naturally.

Reactive bloom: consume prepared seeds for immediate recovery, then spend time and mana replanting.		Sustained healing is slower to rescue; consuming seeds trades future healing for current health.
Druid / StarcallerAlternate solar and lunar spells to control a Cycle state, then release the appropriate spender.Alternating balance: use both spell families to maintain access to repeated balanced finishes.

Committed phase: stay in one phase longer for stronger short output before an explicit recovery phase.		Balance loses maximum phase burst; commitment is more vulnerable to target loss and forced movement.
Shaman / StormcallerAlternate elemental attacks and release Stormcharge through a line, field or priority-target strike.Focused discharge: pool for a high-value target with limited chaining.

Distributed storm: spend through fields and capped chains against groups.		Focus loses area output; field damage requires enemies to remain inside the area.
Shaman / SpiritmenderCreate temporary links with direct heals and choose a limited path for later recovery.Close kinship: heal a compact group through efficient short chain jumps.

Focused memory: spend links on protection and direct recovery of threatened recipients.		Chains lose value when allies spread; focused protection reduces group throughput.
Shaman / TotemistAllocate four static-object capacity units and define at most three links; placement and timing determine damage and support.Anchored constellation: retain a stable layout for sustained area effects.

Portable pattern: relocate a smaller layout more often with lower peak output.		Anchoring loses value against movement; portability sacrifices stationary uptime and throughput.
Monk / IronbodyUse a defensive stance to delay a bounded part of damage, then spend Balance to reduce the debt; delayed damage is still owed.Debt management: prepare mitigation and clear part of the ledger before a dangerous overlap.

Reactive countering: earn Balance through timed defense and accept a smaller delayed-damage pool.		A large ledger risks delayed death; reactive defense is less reliable when timing fails.
Monk / WindfistAlternate distinct martial actions to preserve Flow while generating and spending Chi.Sustained combinations: preserve varied chains and distribute spending.

Burst sequences: build a short complete sequence and spend heavily on a bounded finisher.		Sustained sequences lose peak burst; burst is punished by interruption and target loss.
Monk / LifewalkerUse direct recovery and close-range techniques to build Living Chi; choose whether entering melee is safe.Martial healing: gain extra Chi from safe attacks while an ally needs recovery.

Protective meditation: heal at short range and preserve a stationary channel for predictable damage.		Martial healing risks melee exposure; meditation loses damage contribution and mobility.
Warlock / DemonologistDirect one major demon and build Authority through combined actions rather than unattended pet damage.Coordinated offense: plan owner/demon attacks and spend Authority in synchronized windows.

Protective contract: use the same command budget for capped interception and wards.		Offense sacrifices protection; protection reduces damage. Losing the demon disrupts both but does not remove the owner's basic spell.
Warlock / HellcallerBuild Infernal Debt through attacks, then discharge it while managing a capped nonlethal personal cost.Controlled furnace: maintain a limited field and repay Debt frequently.

Risked discharge: pool Debt for a larger attack with explicit health/defense exposure.		Controlled repayment loses peak burst; larger debt risks death from enemy pressure even though the self-cost itself is nonlethal.
Warlock / PactbinderMaintain one contract clause; create progress through paid, successful actions and decide when to settle it.Personal contract: spend more of the contract budget on individual damage.

Shared liability: fund capped allied protection and debuffs at a personal-output cost.		Personal output loses support; allied value depends on recipients acting and taking relevant damage.

What to remove during playtesting

Remove nodes that require using an inappropriate heal on a healthy target for damage, spamming cancels to generate resources, swapping equipment mid-combat for free protection, or standing in enemy damage solely to manufacture resource loops. Remove numerical filler when it does not support pacing, resource thresholds or a meaningful branch choice. The 36-node layout is a capacity solution for the requested point budget, not proof that every node deserves to survive testing.
