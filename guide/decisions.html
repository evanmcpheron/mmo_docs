Design decisions

Mechanics, governing rules, edge cases and acceptance criteria.

This revision supersedes the supplied v1 character pack. It is a gameplay-design reference and numerical prototype baseline, not a shipping game, a balance certification, or a complete Godot implementation. Structural checks and arithmetic tests are distinguished from tests that require a playable server.

Confirmed direction

Area v2 rule
Presentation 2D top-down pixel art; eight sprite orientations; continuous movement and targeting
Combat Hybrid target-based combat with directional attacks, ground effects and server-owned outcomes
Roles Tank, healer, damage; support is a substantial secondary identity, not a required fourth slot
Group baseline Five players: one tank, one healer, three damage specializations
Character scope Twelve classes, 37 specializations, level cap 60
Character creation Very similar to World of Warcraft in layout, presentation and flow: character preview, faction/race/class, appearance, name, review and entry
Beginner starting area Guided, solo-completable level 1–10 area, ending with specialization and a clear next destination; also the first complete playable testing ground
Talent awards Two points per level-up, from level 2 through 60: one class and one specialization point
Final talent budget 59 class + 59 specialization = 118 points; no extra points at character creation
Specialization Select at level 10; previously earned specialization points become spendable then
Races Eleven distinct races; Humans may join either side; Ogres removed
Faction restrictions Paladin and Cleric: good-aligned side only. Necromancer and Warlock: evil-aligned side only. Other classes: both
Professions Two manufacturing slots, two gathering slots, and three common life skills; progression is separate from combat talents
Persistence Items, currency, crafting orders and build changes use authoritative, versioned records
Excluded Fictional history, place names, capitals, religion, biographies, race cultures, authored story quests and detailed maps. The beginner-area feature flow and learning objectives are included.

“Good” and “evil” are working faction-selection labels, not a morality simulation. Race and faction are separate fields. Nothing in this revision creates a crime/alignment meter, forces moral dialogue choices, or assigns new stories to a race.

What changed from v1

The previous 50-point progression is retired. Giving 118 points to the old 18-node trees would remove meaningful scarcity and exceed their maximum selectable ranks. The new tree layout contains 36 nodes per tree, with 92 purchasable ranks in any legal fully expanded route and only 59 available points. Early exclusive transformations begin in tier II; capstones remain late-game choices.

The former assumption that every faction receives every class is also retired. Mandatory encounter capabilities instead have shared providers. This is functional coverage, not a promise that faction-exclusive classes have identical mechanics or that faction populations will balance themselves.

V1's original 330 ability identifiers are retained for traceability. Their unlock schedules, execution profiles and review status are documented in the ability catalog. The original 882 talent identifiers remain traceable in the revised catalog alongside 882 additional nodes. Generic or contradictory wording is corrected where detected; bespoke talent interactions that still need implementation binding are explicitly marked rather than silently treated as finished code.

Decision precedence

  1. These v2 global rules and explicit v2 overrides govern.
  2. A specific ability or talent contract may override a default only through a named field.
  3. A legacy intent sentence never overrides a resource cap, targeting permission, faction restriction, crowd-control rule or transaction invariant.
  4. Missing behavior is rejected by content validation; it is not guessed by a client or runtime AI.

Recommended boundaries

Keep class changes disabled. A specialization switch changes role and selected tree, not class. Race changes and faction transfers are not a launch feature; character creation must validate the final race/faction/class combination. Do not quietly convert a faction-exclusive class during transfer.

Keep the bazaar faction-local at first, including crafting orders. Both sides receive equivalent recipes, material access rules and NPC fallback services. Cross-faction listings, neutral exchanges and war logistics are deferred mechanics; no supply chain depends on them.

Account-wide collections may be cosmetic. Do not add account-wide combat power, profession throughput boosts or unlimited prestige statistics in this baseline.

What “complete” means here

All requested systems have a proposed policy, key numerical defaults, interactions, persistence rules and acceptance criteria. The inherited content inventory remains navigable. The numerical execution profiles are explicit starting points, but several named secondary effects and transformative talent bindings still require individual implementation work. The audit lists these as open content-engineering gates. No page claims all 330 abilities or all talent combinations have been simulated or balanced.
