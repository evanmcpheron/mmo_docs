Briarwake: world and story

An original frontier setting organized around rebuilding useful paths.

Premise

Recommended original setting: the Briarwake is a recurring surge of unnatural growth around abandoned waystones. It exposes old paths and ruins while disturbing creatures and supply routes. Settlements survive through skilled outings, preparation and restoration—not by waiting for a chosen hero to solve everything alone in a cutscene.

The frontier’s history

Long before Thornmere, a network of signal bells and maintained roads linked the basin, forest, fen, heights, mountain and coast. Its keepers disagreed over whether the waystones should be sealed or studied. The neglected network now fails in different ways across the six regions. The player discovers that repairing useful links and understanding the warnings matters as much as defeating the immediate threats.

Independent civic organizations

Retain these original names and service roles. Their relationship values are civic reputation, not the mandatory permanent FactionId. The Hearth Assembly is independent of the Hearthward League.

Six connected regional arcs

Thornmere teaches the systems. Elderwood joins the vocation contributions at North Road and Bellroot Vault. Siltfen tests water/food/research choices. Cairnstep emphasizes supply and supports. Hollowpeak develops salvage and old plans. Galehaven concludes the route network through beacon/ferry restoration.

Permanent history and repeatable adventure

Major construction remains complete. Repeatable threats, maintenance contracts, new discoveries and optional reinforcement provide renewed activity without resetting a village to punish absent players. First-arrival recognition is optional; later players retain meaningful personal arcs.

Content boundaries

The six-quest Hearthward opening is now authored in the canonical campaign source, including dialogue and localization export. Its maps, runtime quests, scene presentation and NPC assets are not implemented by this documentation change. Complete campaigns for the other three political factions and later regional story production remain future work. No Tolkien characters, geography, plots or distinctive designs are part of the specification.

Political history and faction perspectives

Before the present waystone disturbance, bell/road maintenance linked communities without settling sovereignty. Human-led frontier charters formed the Hearthward League to restore reliable roads and defend settlements; some charters ignored earlier woodland and ridge claims. Briarbound councils protected sacred places and required negotiated access. Ironfang warbands asserted ancestral territory and self-determination, divided over restraint and raids. Tidebound harbor compacts resisted imposed tolls and external control of coastal infrastructure. These are political conflicts, not good/evil species. The Hollow Covenant exploits the waystone disturbance; its threat can require uneasy cooperation without erasing disagreements.

Roadward Accord, Hearth Assembly and Veil Survey remain independent cross-faction civic organizations with their own reputation. Hearthward League is not a renamed Hearth Assembly. Thornmere, Elderwood, Siltfen, Cairnstep, Hollowpeak and Galehaven retain their existing arcs, hubs, landmarks and construction. The new Hearthward opening provides a personal route into those same stories rather than replacing them.
