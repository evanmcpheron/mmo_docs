    Pixel clusters, outlines and shading

    Build readable masses before texture, and use each pixel to clarify shape or material.

    Edition 0.1.0 · Owner review pending for new reference art

    On this page
    1. Cluster construction
    2. Enlarged cluster practice
    3. Selective one-pixel outlines
    4. Four purposeful values
    5. Repair a failed sprite

    Cluster construction

    Draw the silhouette as a single mass, divide it into lit and shadow planes, then add only clusters that describe curvature or a material break. A useful leaf cluster is a connected 3–8-pixel shape with a stepped edge, not a scatter of single bright dots. At 1×, each cluster should join a larger canopy mass.

    Remove isolated pixels unless they explain an eye, fastening, spark or one-pixel edge correction. Avoid repeated diagonal stair steps that form unintended stripes. Check negative space between legs, under arms and around tools. One pixel can open a pose; ten extra highlights can close it.

    Enlarged cluster practice

    Use the viewer’s 8× zoom and pixel grid to inspect connected shapes; use 1× to decide whether they work. The grass tile places short connected blades within broad quiet color. The tree repeats broad lobes with irregular silhouette breaks. Neither requires a different shade on every leaf.

    A good practice pass removes half the interior texture and keeps the form intact. If removing texture destroys recognition, improve the silhouette. If repeated terrain forms a checker pattern, lower interior contrast or offer compatible alternatives rather than hiding the tile boundary with more noise.

    Selective one-pixel outlines

    Start small characters with selective one-pixel colored outlines. Use deep blue, green or brown shadows related to the material; keep true black exceptional. Darken the lower-right edge and gear overlap, and open or lighten the upper-left edge where the form meets bright light.

    Interior outlines separate an arm from a tunic or a blade from a torso. They should disappear where surfaces share the same plane. Against bright snow, preserve a dark-side contour. Against dark forest ground, retain a light-facing shoulder or hair edge. Do not solve every background by enclosing every object in solid black.

    Four purposeful values

    Use three to five meaningful shades per ordinary material as an initial guideline. In the study ramps, shadow separates, shade turns the plane, body carries local color and light names the lit plane. Shift hue with value: cool blue-green shadows beneath warmer yellow-green leaves, cooler metal shade beneath a pale reflection.

    Assume a soft upper-left daylight direction for baked form shading. Runtime lights add broad local mood without moving every painted highlight. Metal uses a small hard light edge; bark uses broken vertical planes; cloth uses larger folds; stone uses irregular flat facets. A metal highlight that covers half the blade reads as white plastic.

    Repair a failed sprite

    1. Flatten the image to a single silhouette and identify ambiguous appendages. Redraw the large shape first.
    2. Reduce material colors into coherent ramps; eliminate near-duplicate values that add no plane change.
    3. Align light-facing planes to upper-left. Keep contact shadows tight beneath objects.
    4. Remove random dithering, double outlines and alternating single-pixel texture. Recheck 1× contrast against grass, stone and snow.
    5. Export at native size and compare without browser smoothing. A blurred preview cannot establish a crisp source.
