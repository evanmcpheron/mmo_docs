    Trees, vegetation and resource props

    A manageable natural library with compatible roots, canopies and density.

    Edition 0.1.0 · Owner review pending for new reference art

    On this page

    2. Ground contact and sorting

    Tree construction standard

    The proposed deciduous study uses a 48×64 canvas with clustered canopy lobes, a 5–8-pixel main trunk and contact near (24,60). The canopy is a visual mass, not the collision shape. Reserve a suggested 2×1-tile root footprint; final collision is reviewed by gameplay. An evergreen uses stacked tapered clusters at the same native density. Dead trees use branch silhouette rather than a leaf texture recolor.

    Paint upper-left highlights in broad connected patches, darker leaf separation at the lower-right and a small ground shadow. Avoid a bright highlight on every leaf. Vary silhouette and canopy lobe placement between trees while retaining trunk scale. A large ancient tree needs a larger canvas and authored structure, not a rescaled small tree.

    Ground contact and sorting

    Deliver the trunk and canopy together for a simple static object, with a ground anchor at the root contact. For roofs or canopies that must reveal characters, split foreground canopy from the trunk while keeping identical coordinates and an explicit registration reference. Keep contact shadows distinct from overhead foliage.

    Y-sort at root contact rather than top-left or image bottom by accident. A character behind the trunk should pass behind it; a character below it should draw in front unless a deliberate overhead canopy covers them. Shared z_index and parent y_sort_enabled conditions require engine verification. Collision should usually cover the trunk base, not the canopy silhouette.

    Sparse, normal and dense placement

    Sparse placement leaves broad grass between one tree, a few stones and an herb patch. Normal placement groups trees in twos or threes around a clear route, with intermittent low props. Dense placement overlaps canopies at the map edge but preserves a visible travel corridor and combat clearance. Density refers to objects per area, not extra texture per tile.

    Build forest, marsh and mountain edges from the same library by changing the ratio and palette relationships: deciduous clusters in forest, dead trunks and reeds in marsh, evergreen and rock in mountains. Recolor variants require palette review and should not erase species silhouette.

    Resource state contract

    A gatherable object needs a readable available and depleted state where gameplay requires it. For ore, remove the bright ore cluster without shifting the rock contact; for berries, keep the bush silhouette and remove fruit; for trees, align the stump with the trunk root. Do not require every decorative object to own resource state.

    Submit the state names, root position and any intended obstacle change. A depleted tree becoming a stump may change collision, but the authoritative resource or world system decides when that occurs. Avoid decorative flowers that use the same highlight as an active gathering marker.
