    Modular buildings and settlements

    Grounded medieval fantasy built from named, reusable pieces.

    Edition 0.1.0 · Owner review pending for new reference art

    A complete building kit

    Start with a common 16×16 module and native density. A kit needs walls, inside and outside corners, foundations, doors, windows, roof centers, roof edges, ridge and eave pieces. Chimneys, shutters, support beams, pillars, stairs, fences and signs are independent additions. Some demonstration pieces share geometry; that does not remove their semantic role from the cell dictionary.

    The study house uses a 5×5-tile visual envelope: a three-row roof, one front wall row and a foundation/stair row. It is a compact construction proof. Production door and wall variants must be extended to the character-clearance standard before runtime approval. Keep roof and body widths compatible instead of stretching an assembled PNG.

    Assembly recipe

    1. Reserve an 80×80 visual envelope on a 5×5 grid. Choose an entrance at column 2 of the front face.
    2. Stamp five ridge cells on roof row 0. On row 1 use left edge, three roof centers and right edge. Stamp eave cells on row 2.
    3. Stamp wall corners at columns 0 and 4 of row 3, windows at 1 and 3 and the doorway at 2. On row 4 use foundations and the centered stair.
    4. Add the chimney above the wall plane and a sign beside the entrance. Record any sorting overlap.
    5. Repeat the same pieces for a second placement; vary frontage accessories and layout before commissioning an entirely new atlas.

    Footprints and connection points

    Record visual bounds, grid footprint, entrance cells and floor threshold separately. A 5×5 exterior envelope is not a 5×5 collision rectangle. Roof overhang and front wall height change the image without changing walkable interior area. Doors connect to paths at whole-cell boundaries. Fences require ends, corners and gate-compatible connections.

    Prepare alternate roof visibility when buildings can contain players. Split a roof into its own aligned layer or sprite, and record which contact line governs sorting. Technical artists test player occlusion and doorway passage. Never bake interface interaction markers permanently into a building sprite.

    Settlement composition

    Group structures around readable circulation. Leave at least a two-cell visual route for ordinary travel as a recommended starting point, then test actual player and collision widths. Keep stalls and foliage from blocking sight to doors. Use signs and one warm window to establish purpose. Repeated buildings should vary frontage and placement rather than pixel density.

    Review both settlement scenes side by side. The second uses the same roof, wall, bridge and prop assets in a new arrangement. A new settlement should first exhaust layout and material variations before adding a large new kit.
