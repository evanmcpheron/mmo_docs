    Art Bible · Foundations

    Art direction and perspective

    Consistent scale, clear action and a world that artists can build from reusable pieces.

    Edition 0.1.0 · Owner review pending for new reference art

    On this page
    1. The visual hierarchy
    2. Top-down camera contract
    3. Storytelling at moderate detail

    The visual hierarchy

    Apply this order during every review: visual consistency, gameplay readability, reusable construction, character identity, environmental atmosphere, then decorative detail. A beautiful sprite with a shifted ground anchor fails before color polish is considered. A road with visible seams fails before flowers are added.

    Aim for a substantial fantasy RPG built from a manageable library. Use broad grass masses, clustered tree canopies, timber and stone structures, deliberate warm windows and restrained landmarks. Ordinary ground stays quieter than bodies, hands, tools and danger indicators.

    Top-down camera contract

    Use an orthogonal, traditional top-down presentation with visible front faces on characters and buildings. Keep verticals vertical. Ground cells remain squares in map space. Roofs show a simple top plane; walls show enough front face to communicate entry height. This is an artistic oblique view without isometric diamond coordinates or cinematic convergence.

    The study buildings use a 5×5-tile visual envelope including roof and front face. Their traversable footprint is a separate plan. Apply the same slope convention within a building kit; never rescale a roof to fit a wall. Cardinal doors must meet ground paths on tile boundaries. Diagonal movement does not rotate the camera or terrain.

    Storytelling at moderate detail

    Tell a workshop story with a broad apron silhouette, an anvil, stacked timber and one warm working light. Use a repaired beam and clean road edge to signal restoration. Avoid lore written in tiny decoration: an artist may suggest age through broken geometry and muted moss without inventing historical events or named factions.

    At 1×, a player should distinguish the route, doorway, resource and enemy before noticing surface grain. Reserve the strongest local contrast for actors and interactables. Each decorative cluster should reinforce mass, material, light or use.

    Rejected treatments

    The comparison moves from grouped foliage to noisy foliage, inconsistent internal pixel size and excessive gradients. Reject each failure on its own: noise competes with action; doubled pixels break density; gradients introduce too many values for a small form. The rejected cells are teaching examples, never production targets.
