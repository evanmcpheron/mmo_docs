    Pixel scale, bounds and anchors

    A sprite cell is a container. Its visible body, contact point and collision are different measurements.

    Edition 0.1.0 · Owner review pending for new reference art

    Native pixels and display pixels

    One native pixel is one sample in the source PNG. At integer display scale 4, that sample occupies a 4×4 block of screen pixels. A 16×16 tile becomes 64×64 screen pixels, and a 32-pixel-tall body becomes 128 screen pixels tall. This does not change either source asset. A 1.5× scale gives alternating sample widths and is unsuitable as a production correction.

    Keep sprite and terrain transforms at the agreed native relationship. A tree drawn with 2×2 chunks masquerading as single pixels does not become compatible by shrinking its node. Redraw the tree with the same one-pixel units as the character.

    A measured coordinate contract

    Recommended character frame coordinates use a top-left origin (0,0), x right and y down. Reserve a 64×64 transparent cell; place standing contact at (32,48). The original study has its head near y17 and feet near y47, so visible height is about 31 pixels while the cell remains 64 pixels tall. Action reach may occupy the margin, but the body must not drift to recenter a sword.

    Bounds are half-open rectangles [x,y,width,height]. An occupied pixel at x37 belongs to a bound ending at x38. The visual union includes hair and equipment; the collision reference covers only the walkable body footprint. Ground contact stays stable through a walk; a jump or dodge may lift the body while its ground shadow and root remain registered.

    World scale ladder

    A humanoid is roughly two terrain tiles tall. A 16-pixel icon is a symbolic inventory view and does not measure the world sword. A small crate is typically 16×16 or 32×32; furniture seats should accept the same body. Doors start at 16 pixels clear width and 32 pixels visible height when using a taller modular door kit. The small atlas doorway is a construction study requiring an extended door for final character clearance.

    Use 16-pixel multiples for large prop canvases and building modules. Tree canopies vary by species; the 48×64 study is a recommended size, not a measured game tree. A boss may exceed a 64-pixel canvas while keeping one-pixel edges and the same material cluster scale.

    Camera and resolution decisions

    Candidate 320×180 shows a compact 20×11.25-tile field; 384×216 shows 24×13.5; 480×270 shows 30×16.875. Fractional tile counts at viewport edges are normal. Compare action reach, readable text, UI occupancy and 30-actor crowd tests before selecting a logical resolution. These are tuning examples, not final settings.

    A 1920×1080 output can display 320×180 at 6×, 384×216 at 5× or 480×270 at 4×. On other screens, use a documented integer viewport scale with letterboxing or a reviewed expanded field. Camera zoom multiplies display scale; 2× camera zoom at 4× viewport scaling yields 8 screen pixels per native pixel. Godot stretch mode and camera policy still need runtime verification.

    Artist measurement procedure

    1. Open the export with a visible one-pixel grid. Count the visible body height, then measure canvas separately.
    2. Overlay a 16×16 grid and the (32,48) contact cross. Compare all frames and all equipment on that cross.
    3. Place character, door, tree, weapon and ground together at 1×. Compare pixels inside each object, not just total size.
    4. Preview integer display scales without resizing sources. Reject filtered, nonuniform or subpixel placement artifacts.
