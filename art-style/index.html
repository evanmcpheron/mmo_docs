    A world made one tile at a time

    Atmospheric medieval fantasy at a practical native scale. Forest paths lead to warm workshops, weathered settlements and dangerous ruins. Characters carry identity through silhouette and equipment; terrain supports the action without demanding attention from every pixel. The influence of Baldur’s Gate 3 concerns worldbuilding, architecture and environmental storytelling, translated into an original traditional top-down RPG.

    Read, inspect, then draw

    1. Start with art direction, pixel scale and drawing techniques. Measure silhouette at 1× before adding detail.
    2. Load the proposed palette, create a native Aseprite document and name layers by visual responsibility. Keep guides out of exports.
    3. Use the relevant terrain, architecture, character or icon chapter. Inspect the downloadable reference metadata and reproduce the demonstrated registration.
    4. Export at 100%, validate source association and review at gameplay scale. Submit an editable source, PNG, metadata and completed handoff.
    5. Art approval and Godot runtime validation are separate gates. A documentation preview is never production approval.

    Publication and approval

    Art Bible version 0.1.0. Published for review 15 October 2026. The owner-approved direction is approachable, atmospheric, moderately detailed, reusable top-down pixel art. Every new palette, sprite and scene in this edition remains proposed or a technical demonstration. Production approval requires a named owner decision and separate technical evidence.

    This Art Bible supersedes earlier Unreal/Paper2D/PaperZD art instructions for new work. Historical engineering pages remain archival context; this task does not convert gameplay code or change networking authority.
