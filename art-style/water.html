    Water, cliffs and traversable edges

    Separate terrain surfaces, animated accents and traversal contracts.

    Edition 0.1.0 · Owner review pending for new reference art

    Water family construction

    Use broad horizontal masses for water. A pond or lake combines a quiet base, a shallow shelf, a deep-water interior and a complete shoreline overlay. Rivers reuse these pieces with directional accent strips. Coastlines use a wider shallow band and optional low-frequency foam. Marsh water lowers contrast and shares reed and mud relationships.

    The study atlas contains a four-frame horizontal water accent loop. Use 160 ms per frame as a tuning example; the reflection must return without a jump. Shallow and deep-water palettes, flow arrows and waterfall motion are production specifications requiring further authored variants; the still water demonstration is not a complete running-water or waterfall kit.

    Elevation without perspective drift

    A cliff face consumes screen height while its top footprint stays on the 16×16 map grid. Use repeatable vertical bands, corner caps and a distinct top rim. Start with a 16-pixel face height as a recommended kit unit; stack bands for taller cliffs rather than scaling one face. A stair strip must meet both lower and upper ground boundaries.

    Show where a character stands at the top and base. The artist supplies visible rim, contact and obstruction suggestions; gameplay decides traversability and actual collision. Avoid painting a dark stripe that reads as an impassable wall if it is meant to be walkable terrain.

    Edges affect gameplay integration

    Deliver a marked preview for collision, navigation, occlusion and interaction separately. A waterfall can be a visual sprite behind a traversable bridge; a shoreline may allow fishing without walking. A canopy or cliff overhang may hide a player and needs a foreground or fade treatment. None of those behaviors are guaranteed by the PNG.

    Bank connection pixels and bridge origins must remain compatible across damaged and restored states. Any changed navigation area must be identified in the handoff, not embedded as an unexplained color change.

    Loop and seam review

    1. Place a 10×10 base water repeat with a full shoreline on each side, including concave bays and isolated islands.
    2. Play water tiles beside the shore: the shore remains registered and reflection accents do not creep across boundaries.
    3. Test the flow strip, crest, waterfall body and splash as separate modules. Match loop duration or document independent periods.
    4. Place a 32-pixel character at banks, stairs, bridge rails and cave mouths. Check visibility at 1× and request engine traversal review.
