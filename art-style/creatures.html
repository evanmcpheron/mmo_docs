    NPCs, enemies, wildlife and bosses

    Readable silhouettes and threat poses at the same native density as players.

    Edition 0.1.0 · Owner review pending for new reference art

    NPC identity before ornament

    Villagers, merchants, guards, travelers and quest NPCs use the humanoid construction contract where appropriate. Distinguish a guard by a strong equipment silhouette and stance; a merchant by service-side props; a traveler by a compact pack or outer layer. Quest importance belongs to interface markers and interaction systems, not excessive permanent glow painted into skin.

    Keep everyday clothing within the material palette and let recognizable headwear or a single accessory carry identity. An NPC near a busy market must still separate from stalls. Do not give every villager a unique scale or brighter color ramp.

    Directional poses and attacks

    Author all required facings for mobile creatures. A quadruped diagonal needs a changed body axis, head position and leg overlap. A larger canvas is justified by anatomy or reach, not by adding finer pixel density. Outline joints selectively so the creature remains readable on grass, mud and stone.

    Separate anticipation, activation and recovery. A wide attack pose can extend beyond contact while the root stays fixed. The art shows intent; authoritative hitboxes and timing remain gameplay-owned. Pair silhouettes with telegraphs that remain visible beneath other effects.

    Crowd and environmental testing

    Compare a player beside a small animal, humanoid enemy and boss. Test one enemy at a time and a party surrounding it. Use the same camera and integer scale as the player studies. Dark fur against marsh ground needs an edge or value separation; pale creatures on snow need a dark-side contour.

    Do not accept an enemy solely on an empty neutral canvas. Include foreground vegetation, water or cliff context, UI markers and overlapping impacts. Record any tell that disappears under effects. Boss details should read as a few large masses when the preview is reduced to gameplay size.

    Delivery contract

    Submit silhouettes, directions, action tags, contact, suggested collision footprint and the largest animation bounds. A boss may need multiple visual components, but splitting limbs must have an explicit assembly and timeline contract. Additional implementation should be justified by the animation, not assumed for every large enemy.
