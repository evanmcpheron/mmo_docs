    Art Bible · Environments

    Interiors, furniture and visibility

    Use the same terrain scale indoors, with clear contact and foreground behavior.

    Edition 0.1.0 · Owner review pending for new reference art

    On this page

    2. Furniture as registered objects
    3. Character visibility and draw order

    Interior grid and floor plans

    Keep the 16×16 grid indoors. Do not enlarge characters or furniture to fill a room. A recommended small workshop plan is 8×6 tiles: one front entrance, a two-cell circulation strip, a two-by-one workbench against the back wall, storage along one side and clear floor for a standing character. A cottage can start at 6×5; an inn common room at 12×8. These are tuning examples rather than canonical buildings.

    Use repeatable wood or stone floor tiles with quiet seams. Back walls may occupy two visual tile rows while their ground contact occupies one boundary. Corner and doorway pieces must preserve the same face depth as the exterior kit. Provide a plan and an assembled preview; a painted room PNG alone is insufficient.

    Character visibility and draw order

    Separate the base floor, ground rug, furniture, back walls, front wall and overhead ceiling/roof. Ground rugs stay below feet. Tables and chairs sort by their contact line. Foreground walls and tall shelves may need a cutaway or fade when the player stands behind them. Artists provide compatible alternate art; programmers control visibility.

    Do not let a decorative foreground silhouette cover interaction prompts or combat tells. Test a 32-pixel body beside every tall item and between chair and table. Keep collision reference guides out of PNG export.

    Light and material consistency

    Use baked upper-left form shading on furniture even when a warm lamp provides runtime mood. Window light can be a reusable overlay with a sharp or deliberately stepped pixel boundary. Avoid soft gradients painted across the floor atlas: they repeat badly and bind a room to one lighting setup.

    Distinguish service objects by silhouette and a small material cue. An alchemy bench may use bottles; a smithing area uses dark metal and a warm heat accent; a cooking area uses wood, stone and cookware. Vocation styling must not invent mandatory gameplay equipment or a new station ownership system.

    Interior handoff

    Submit the floor/wall atlas, independent furniture sources, one room recipe, contact points and a visibility plan. Name intended doors and windows without changing canonical map or lore IDs. Final Godot review checks the complete entrance transition, sorting and UI legibility in a populated room.
